# Limitations

An honest list. Some are design decisions, some are unfinished work, some are the price of targeting one platform.

## Cameras and files

- **The rarer raw formats are untested.** ARI, BAY, BMQ, CAP, CINE, CS1, DC2, DCR, ERF, GPR, IA, K25, KC2, KDC, MDC, MEF, PXN, QTK, RAW, RDC, RW1 and STI are catalogued because LibRaw reads them, but none has been opened in Latent on a real file; many come from cameras with sensors Latent doesn't render (below).
- **Bayer sensors only.** Fujifilm X-Trans, Sigma Foveon, monochrome and four-colour sensors are read for metadata but do not render.
- **Colour checked on one camera.** The golden-image tests, which compare renders with reference images, use a Nikon D750 NEF. The Photo Merge tests also read raws from a Canon EOS 5D Mark II and a Nikon D200, but don't check their colour. LibRaw supports hundreds of cameras and the pipeline is generic, but colour and exposure on other cameras have not been checked against a reference.
- **No built-in camera-matching profiles.** Latent Standard renders from the camera's characterisation matrix, so it will not match the in-camera JPEG look. Camera-matching looks come from DNG camera profiles (.dcp) you import yourself: Latent bundles none and downloads none. Triple-illuminant profiles (DNG 1.6) are read as dual-illuminant ones, a profile's tone curve is applied on top of Latent's tone mapping rather than instead of it, and there is no Adaptive profile, Profile Browser with previews, favourites or LUT-based creative profiles.
- **JPEG, HEIC, TIFF and other pictures are SDR and flattened.** An HDR gain map in a phone's HEIC or JPEG is ignored and the SDR picture opens; a floating-point or HDR TIFF is clipped at white. Layers, PSD and PSB files, and multi-page TIFFs beyond the first page aren't read, nor JPEG XL on a Mac whose macOS can't decode it. A picture's edits are Latent's own and live in its sidecar; they are not written into the file, and other applications won't see them.
- **Lens profiles** cover the bundled Lensfun subset. A lens the matcher cannot identify gets no profile automatically; pick one by hand from the Lens Corrections panel, or rely on the manual sliders and Remove chromatic aberration. Profiles remembered for a lens are kept per catalog, and `latent-cli` doesn't read them.
- **HDR in exports is a gain map or PQ.** JPEG, HEIC and AVIF can carry an HDR gain map; HEIC and AVIF can instead be full HDR (10-bit Rec. 2100 PQ) with Maximize Compatibility off. The headroom is the photo's HDR Limit (at most three stops); there are no separate settings for the SDR rendition, no HLG, and full HDR falls back to a gain map with a watermark, output sharpening or a converted colour space. PNG, TIFF and JPEG XL are SDR only, and a gain map is written only in sRGB or Display P3.
- **DNG export bakes the corrections, or the look under a curve.** By default a DNG holds white balance, exposure, noise reduction, lens corrections, healing and masks, with tone, colour grading, presence and sharpening left out. Full look baked in stores the whole edit under the inverse of Latent's default tone curve: exact in Latent, approximate in other raw converters. Original raw as DNG copies DNGs, but writes other raws as a demosaiced linear DNG, not a mosaic-preserving conversion. There is no Embed fast load data or Embed original raw. Keep a DNG's white balance at As Shot: the pixels are already balanced.
- **JPEG XL export needs a macOS with an encoder.** AVIF and JPEG XL can be chosen only when this Mac's system can write them; otherwise they are listed greyed out. macOS 27.2 has no JPEG XL encoder, so JPEG XL export is untested on a real file. There is no PSD.
- **Export formats have edges.** OpenEXR is scene-linear only (the look left out), always full size, in linear sRGB primaries (macOS writes no Rec. 2020 chromaticities into an EXR), with no metadata, border or watermark. A 32-bit float TIFF is the 16-bit render widened, so it holds no highlights above white. PDF images are stored losslessly, so files are large, and a combined PDF is only moved into place once every photo is in it: a cancelled run leaves no partial file. A bordered export is SDR only: HDR Output is left out. Greyscale when monochrome judges by the edit (Black & White on, no colour grading or tint), not the pixels, so a colour mask on a black and white edit is turned grey too. There is no size estimate for PDF, and the one for OpenEXR is approximate. An embedded edit can only be read back by code (`ExportMetadata.embeddedEdit(in:)`); there is no menu command yet to load it.
- **No XMP beside the raw while you work.** Latent's sidecars live in `_latent/xmp/`, where Lightroom and darktable do not look. To hand a raw to another application with its rating, label and keywords, export it as **Original + XMP sidecar**.
- **HDR display** has been verified on a MacBook Pro's XDR screen, and sRGB and Display P3 output on the same machine. Other HDR displays are untested.
- **Soft proofing has two intents,** Perceptual and Relative Colorimetric (with black point compensation unless you turn it off); Saturation isn't offered. Black point compensation treats the paper's black as neutral. Profiles in your own ~/Library/ColorSync/Profiles aren't listed by themselves: the sandbox lets Latent read one only after you pick it with **Add Profile…**. The soft-proof target and options are the same for every photo, not remembered per photo. The print panel can't choose a profile of its own: it prints into the soft proof's while proofing is on. The monitor gamut warning reads the profile of the screen the window was on when the warning was switched on; move the window to another screen and switch it off and on again. Proof copies are snapshots, not virtual copies, so only one version of a photo shows and exports at a time; to keep a screen version and a print version side by side, make a virtual copy and proof that.

## Platform and distribution

- **Apple Silicon and macOS 15 only.** No Intel Macs, no Windows, no Linux, by design.
- **Not notarised.** Without an Apple developer account, every other Mac shows the Gatekeeper dialog once.
- **Large.** The four bundled machine-learning models alone are 311 MB, 103 MB of it the BiRefNet Lite subject model. Models added from disk take their own space: up to 457 MB each for the largest in the catalogue, and a second or more to load; on an 8 GB Mac the bundled ones are the safer choice.
- **Neural Engine off by default** because its compiler hangs on some macOS 15 builds. The GPU is fast enough.

## Workflow

- **No Lightroom catalog import.** Latent can't read a Lightroom catalog (`.lrcat`), its collections or its develop settings. From the `.xmp` files Lightroom, Capture One and darktable write beside raws it reads ratings, reject flags, labels, keywords and descriptive fields only, never edits, and never writes to them; darktable's duplicates (`DSC_0107_01.NEF.xmp`) are not read, and of several darktable colour labels only the first is kept. See [Ratings and keywords from other apps](Library#ratings-and-keywords-from-other-apps).
- **No import from memory cards, no tethering.** Copy files yourself, or let **Auto Import** move them in from a watched folder that another app saves into. Auto Import takes only the files directly in the watched folder, not its subfolders, and can't be undone.
- **Folder watching misses some changes on network shares.** Changes made from another computer may not be noticed; use File › Refresh Folder (⌘R). The sidebar's folder tree isn't watched: a folder made in Finder appears when its parent is expanded again.
- **No Locate Missing Photo.** A photo deleted or moved out of the folder leaves the catalog; its sidecar stays, so putting the file back restores its rating and edits.
- **Folders on read-only volumes can't be opened,** because the catalog lives inside the folder. A locked memory card has to be copied first.
- **Smart collection limits.** Rules are read strictly from the top down, with no brackets. "Edit changes" compares each part of an edit with Latent's own defaults, so a part a camera's defaults change (a lens profile switched on, say) counts as changed; it and "Edit date" are read from the catalog in the background, so a smart collection using them catches up a moment after an edit is saved. The value menus list at most 200 values. The Recent collections list is kept per folder on this Mac only.
- **Collections belong to one folder; no search across folders.** Each folder is its own catalog with its own collections, and a collection can't hold photos from another folder. The sidebar finds folders by name and moves between them, but doesn't search photos across folders or combine them. A photo moved out of the folder leaves its collections, and undoing the move doesn't put it back in them.
- **The sandbox limits the sidebar** to folders inside a favourite. Anything else needs Open Folder, or adding a folder that contains it.
- **Subfolder include/independent choices are not stored in sidecars.** They live only in the catalog database, so rebuilding the catalog, including the automatic rebuild of a damaged database, resets them and asks again. Changing the choice moves no existing sidecars or thumbnails.
- **Verify Files keeps only verified and damaged results.** A file that couldn't be read or was missing during a check counts as unchecked for the File integrity filter and rule. Results live in the catalog database only, so a rebuilt catalog starts with nothing verified. A DNG's own embedded checksum (the raw image digest Lightroom's Validate DNG Files reads) isn't checked; the whole-file hash covers the same ground once the catalog has seen the file intact. A file damaged before Latent first catalogued it can't be told apart, since its recorded hash is of the damaged file.
- **Find Duplicates looks within the open folder** (and its included subfolders), not across folders, and the same-moment tier goes by the capture time to the second, so a burst at more than one frame a second can show as likely duplicates.
- **No video, no plugins.**
- **Metadata editing** covers rating, flag, colour label, keywords, title, caption, creator, copyright, headline, location (sublocation, city, state, country and its code), the creator's job title, email and web address, and instructions. The creator's postal address and phone, usage terms, and IPTC Extension fields can't be edited. Metadata presets hold descriptive fields only (not keywords or ratings) and live in Latent's settings, not in the folder. GPS positions can be set from a GPX track (Geotag from GPX Track…) but not typed in, and there is no map. Colour labels use the five standard names; there are no label sets. Keywords have no synonyms and no keyword sets. Keyword lists import and export as plain paths only (Lightroom's indented keyword-list format, with its synonyms and export flags, isn't read). Dragging a keyword in the Keyword List moves it only in the open folder and the keyword dictionary; other folders keep the old path until you rename it there.
- **Presets have limits of their own.** Develop › Apply Preset applies a preset plainly; Overwrite and Apply as New Virtual Copy are in the Presets panel, Library panel and grid menus. Only your own presets can be given a key, not the built-in ones. Manage Presets can take groups out of a preset but not add one it never saved (Update from Current Edit captures the groups it already has). There is no hover preview of a preset, and no auto-applied presets for new photos.
- **A preset at export or print is not previewed.** The print panel's preview is drawn from thumbnails, so a print-only preset or print sharpening shows only on paper; Export Open Image doesn't use the export sheet's preset.
- **Finder tags are read, never written.** A tag changed in Finder shows when the folder is next scanned: at once while it is watched, otherwise after Refresh Folder.
- **Undo in the Library has edges.** It covers ratings, flags, labels, titles, captions, creators and copyrights, rotation, keywords, collections, pasted settings and presets, Custom sort rearrangements, virtual copies, stacks, and moves, copies, duplicates and renames. Exports and Auto Import can't be undone. Opening another folder clears it, except for moves, copies and renames. A move, copy or rename can't be undone while an export, print, contact sheet or Photo Merge is running.
- **Mirror Image Mode mirrors the grid and Loupe only.** The filmstrip, Compare, Survey, Develop, the second display and slideshows show photos the right way round, and the mode isn't remembered between launches. Flipping a selection outside Develop skips photos whose crop or masks predate the active-area change until they are opened in Develop once.
- **Aspect ratio sorting ignores the crop**, and Edit count counts the steps the history keeps (it is capped), not every change ever made. There is no Lightroom Media Type sort, since Latent catalogues no video.
- **Not every background job can be paused.** A Photo Merge, a print, a contact sheet and a watched-folder import have no Pause (Auto Import can be switched off in Settings instead), and a paused job carries on from where it was only while Latent stays open: quitting stops it, and nothing is resumed at the next launch. A paused export or selection job still keeps the Mac from sleeping, as a running one does. Nothing waits in a queue: a job that needs the graphics processor while another holds it is refused, not queued.
- **Culling shortcuts are simpler than Lightroom's.** Select by Rating picks exactly that many stars (no "or more" and no add, subtract or intersect with the selection), and the Select commands and Refine Photos work only in the grid.
- **Save Filter as Smart Collection carries the original criteria only.** The rating stars as At least, flags, Edited, camera, lens, keyword, Finder tag and the file-name search go into the smart collection; the rating operator, excluded labels, Unedited, No Keywords, the ranges, the date range, the kinds and the all-fields search don't, and the Label menu's choices never did. Save them as a filter preset instead.
- **The filter bar's search keeps its old meaning by default.** Words, phrases and -exclusions apply only once the magnifying glass is set to All Searchable Fields; the default search still looks for the whole text in file names, titles and captions. Ranges' histograms are worked out when the popover opens and don't follow photos added while it is open.
- **The info line and watermark don't know keywords.** `{keywords}` is empty in the caption under the image and in the export sheet's name preview; exported names and watermarks have them. The caption's position applies to Loupe and Compare, not Survey or the second display.
- **Renaming files on import** isn't possible, since there is no import step; Rename with Template renames photos already in the folder.
- **Rename, Move and Copy work only in the Library grid,** and not while an export, print, contact sheet or Photo Merge is running.
- **A move takes the raw and its paired JPEG only.** An `.xmp` sidecar another application put beside the raw stays where it was.
- **A move to another disk copies, then deletes the original.** If Latent crashes or is force-quit during a long copy, a hidden `.latent-transfer-…` file can be left in the destination. The original is still in place, and the hidden file can be deleted.
- **Stacks are not written to sidecars.** They live only in the folder's catalog database, so a rebuilt catalog (including the automatic rebuild of a damaged one) starts without them, and another app sees no stacks. There is no stacking by visual similarity, no keys to move a photo up or down within its stack, and the stack chip can't be clicked to expand a stack; use S. An export named like its raw and saved beside it (Same folder as original with the {name} template) counts as the raw's JPEG companion, so it stays hidden while companions are, even when added to the stack.
- **Arranging the Custom order needs dragging.** There are no keys for it.
- **Version names live in the catalog only.** They aren't written to the copy's sidecar, so a rebuilt catalog loses them; they show in the Versions panel, not on the grid's copy mark or in export naming, and Set Copy as Master leaves each row its own name. A master can't be named.
- **Batch edit commands have no default keys.** Reset Edits, Sync Last Change, Paste Settings (Overwrite), Copy Settings…, Paste Settings…, Load Settings from File… and New Blank Copy are menu items; Settings › Keyboard can give them keys (⌥⇧⌘V is the system's Paste and Match Style).
- **Sync Last Change to Selection has edges.** It reads the lead's last step as the history kept it, writes stored edits only (so in Develop it is undone from the Library), and doesn't run the face search or Upright analysis that a paste of Touch-up or Upright Mode starts. Paste Settings (Overwrite) and Reset Edits drop a photo's found faces with its touch-up.
- **Load Settings from File reads Latent's own edit only.** Not Lightroom's or darktable's develop settings, and Latent's exports don't carry their edit yet, so in practice it reads Latent sidecars.
- **The split line's position isn't remembered;** each launch starts in the middle.
- **Typed values aren't on every slider.** Highlights, Shadows, Whites and Blacks, HSL, the B&W Mix, the luminance sliders under the three small colour wheels and export quality show their value but can't be typed into. Temperature takes a typed Kelvin value.
- **The pointer readout and the targeted tools read the picture as shown.** They sample the screen render, so at fit one readout pixel covers several of the photo's, and the tone curve's targeted tool traces the tone back through the curve as it stands, which after HSL or colour grading is close rather than exact. The White Balance Selector reads the raw data itself and is not affected.
- **`latent-cli export` writes images only.** No DNG or Original + XMP, no Same folder as original, Add to This Catalog, stacking, Open in app or several presets in one run, and of colour spaces only sRGB, Display P3 and Adobe RGB from flags (a preset can name ProPhoto or an ICC file). Models you added in the app may not be found by `latent-cli`; masks made with one then use the bundled model of its kind, and it says so. A folder's photos are the ones its catalog lists; run the app or `latent-cli catalog` first to pick up new files.
- **Catalog backups are of the database only.** They don't include sidecars, thumbnails or `custom-order.json`, and are kept inside `_latent/` with the photos, so they don't help if the folder itself is lost. There are five, fixed; there is no setting for how many or how often, and no Back Up Now button. Restoring is for the open folder only.
- **Export open image… is always full size in sRGB.** Use the export sheet for anything else.
- **Compare's and Survey's Sync aren't remembered;** they are on again at every launch.
- **Survey shows at most four images** and has no before/after view. It is for looking; to edit, open an image in Develop.
- **The Loupe on a second display is for looking only.** It shows one image at fit; zoom, pan and tools stay in the main window.
- **Edit in External Editor is a one-way hand-off.** It writes a 16-bit Display P3 TIFF (no Adobe RGB or ProPhoto), and the TIFF the other application saves doesn't come back by itself. Saved into a catalogued folder, it appears in the Library, as a picture of its own, once that folder is read again.
- **Slideshows are SDR,** even on an HDR screen, and leave out AI noise reduction so each slide is ready in time. The edit's other noise reduction still applies.
- **The watermark is for exports.** Prints, contact sheets and the TIFF for an external editor don't carry it. It is one line of text or one PNG graphic, in a corner, turned in quarter turns.
- **A JPEG or PNG contact sheet is one picture,** at most 8192 px a side, holding every photo, so a large selection gets small cells. A PDF can have as many pages as it needs.
- **No Smart Previews and no larger cached previews.** Thumbnails are 512 px, with no size setting, and Loupe and Develop always render from the raw, so a folder on a disconnected drive can't be browsed or edited. There is no disk cache of decoded raws either: the demosaiced image is kept in memory only while a photo is open. See [Thumbnails and performance](Library#thumbnails-and-performance).
- **The print panel's preview is drawn from thumbnails.** It shows the layout; the photos themselves are rendered for the printer only when you print.
- **Showing and hiding the sidebar and filmstrip has no keyboard shortcut or menu item.** The filmstrip has its film button in the status bar.
- **Custom keys have edges.** Settings › Keyboard changes the keys of commands in the menus and the single-key table, not of standard items (Quit, Copy, Settings and the like), gestures, or keys inside panels and sheets (Esc in a dialog, ↑ ↓ in a slider's value). Two commands can't share keys even where they never apply at once, since single keys are looked up by keys alone. Keys are limited to letters, digits, punctuation, Space, the arrows, Return, ⌫, Esc and F2; other function keys and Tab can't be recorded, and Esc on its own cancels recording (a command whose default is Esc gets it back with its reset button). Panning takes single keys only, having no menu item. Menu titles that show a single key, such as Pick (P), follow the change; tooltips in the Develop panels that name a key may still show the default.

## Photo Merge

- **HDR, Panorama and HDR Panorama.** HDR Panorama (⌃⇧M) is **experimental**: it works and is tested end to end, but only against synthetic sweeps and against overlapping windows cut out of a real bracket, because no real HDR panorama exists to check it with. A real bracketed sweep — changing light, parallax across positions, brackets that drift — has never been tried. Focus stacking isn't planned (Lightroom doesn't have it either).
- **Panoramas are one row.** One sweep, left to right or right to left. Several rows stacked into a grid, and full 360° panoramas that join back to their start, aren't built.
- **Parallax.** When the camera moves sideways instead of turning on the spot, near things shift against far things and no stitch lines both up: edges close to the camera can look doubled. The dialog warns when the photos only match to several pixels.
- **A panorama too big to edit is made smaller, never refused.** The dialog says what it would have been, what it will be and why (memory, or the largest picture the graphics processor can hold), and waits for you to agree. The limit is this Mac's: the same sweep merges larger on a bigger machine.
- **A photo that can't be joined is left out** and named in a warning; the panorama is made from the rest. The usual cause is too little overlap — aim for about 30%.
- **Auto Align moves whole frames.** It lines up handheld brackets, but near and far things that shifted against each other (parallax) still show slightly doubled edges. A frame it can't align is merged unaligned if it looks within a pixel or so, or left out of the merge otherwise; the dialog says which.
- **Deghost compares brightness and colour,** and takes each moving area whole from one frame, so something that moved in front of an equally bright background is caught. What it can't fix is parallax, and deghosted areas come from a single frame, with its noise. The dialog's preview and its deghost overlay are small, about 1,000 pixels across, and past a long edge of about 6,100 pixels (roughly 25 MP and up) the overlay is a rougher guide to what the merge takes from one frame.
- **Raw files from Bayer sensors only,** all from one camera at one size and orientation. An already-merged photo can't be merged to HDR again, though a panorama will stitch HDR merges (which is how HDR Panorama works). An HDR merge takes at most 9 photos, or 5 on an 8 GB Mac; a panorama has no frame limit and is made smaller instead of refused.
- **A panorama's lens corrections need a lens Latent can identify.** Distortion, vignetting and chromatic aberration are baked into the stitch, but only when the bundled Lensfun subset matches the lens. When it doesn't, the panorama is stitched uncorrected, nothing warns about it, and because the result is recorded as already corrected, Develop won't correct it afterwards either.
- **Edits on the bracket's photos aren't used.** The merge reads the raw files; the result starts unedited at the reference photo's exposure.
- **The merge options are few, and differ by kind.** HDR offers Auto Align, Deghost (with Show Deghost Overlay, which isn't remembered) and Auto Settings, plus a reference photo you pick that lasts for that merge only — HDR Merge Without Dialog always chooses the reference automatically. Panorama offers Projection, Auto Crop and Auto Settings. HDR Panorama offers both sets. There is nothing else to tune.
- **No re-merge.** The result records which photos made it, but Latent can't merge them again from that record, and a merge can't be undone except by deleting its DNG in Finder.
- **Merged DNGs are large and uncompressed,** about 6 bytes per pixel (roughly 145 MB for 24 MP). Export to DNG writes the same kind of file.
- **A merged DNG copied in from Finder without its sidecar** is catalogued as a plain DNG; the record of what made it stays inside the file, unread.

## Editing

- **Gradients and radials are moved and turned on the image.** A linear gradient is moved by its middle handle and turned by dragging either end; a radial is moved by dragging inside it and turned by the ring above it or the **Angle** slider. A linear gradient has no Angle slider of its own.
- **Masks have no Moiré or colour tint swatch of their own,** no pen pressure and no depth range. A mask's Curve, Point Colour and Grain run after the global edit (with Texture and Clarity), not where Lightroom puts them, so global sharpening also sharpens a mask's grain. A mask holds at most four Point Colour swatches, and Auto Mask compares with the unedited raw, not the picture as edited; Select People chooses everyone, not one person or one body part.
- **Class masks are soft-edged** at the model's native 128-pixel resolution.
- **Mask presets are simple.** They have no folders, no import or export command (copy the files in `mask-presets` instead) and no thumbnails, and a mask preset can't be applied to several photos at once (Copy Mask and Paste Mask to Selection can). Pasting a mask leaves out a part that links to another mask of the source photo, even when pasting back onto the same photo (use Duplicate there). Pick Range samples last for the session only; the edit keeps just the band they made.
- **Mask shape handles work only while the shape's tool is armed,** and only a mouse wheel changes a shape's feather by scrolling: a trackpad's scroll keeps panning. A linear gradient's handles move its ends; it can't be curved, and a radial has no corner handles. **Show Channel** is for range components only, not for the older Luminance range and Hue range tickboxes, and **Pick Range** reads the picture without local adjustments at a reduced size, so a single click averages a few pixels.
- **Mask blending has two modes,** Colour Only and Lightness Only, not darktable's full list (multiply, screen, overlay and the rest), and no blend opacity apart from Amount. Linked masks are one level deep, a link counts every part of the mask it uses toward the six components, and deleting a mask leaves the masks that used it with a Missing Mask link that does nothing. Edge refine works on brush and model masks only, at the masks' quarter resolution, and re-filters the whole mask on every dab while painting with it on. Smooth reaches about 20 pixels of the raw and is a 9 × 9-tap approximation of a bilateral filter, not a full one.
- **Spot healing copies the source's texture.** Tone and colour are matched to the surroundings, but a source with coarser or different texture than the target still shows; drag to pick a better source, or use Remove, which needs none. A brush stroke heals from one source beside it along its whole length, so an edge running along the source, such as a horizon, shows as a line. A stroke keeps up to 256 points; a longer scribble is simplified.
- **Remove fills from the photo itself, not with a generative model.** It continues textures and edges well but cannot invent what is hidden, such as the covered half of a face or lettering; very large areas, and areas with little around them to copy, fill less convincingly. A Remove patch covers at most 35% of the short side and counts toward the 32 patches. Feather does nothing in Remove, and Opacity mixes the fill evenly over the whole area rather than fading it at the edge. Lightroom's Remove tool also has a generative mode and a Refresh button for another fill; Latent's fill is the same every time for the same patch, and has no generative mode. Detect Objects looks at the photo without your edits, so it still sees an object another patch has already removed.
- **Red-eye Auto can miss small faces.** It looks for faces on a render about 1600 px across, so faces in a large group shot may not be found; add those spots by hand. Only red pupils change: an animal's green, yellow or white eyeshine is left as it is, and Pet Eye has no Auto; circle each pet's pupil by hand.
- **AI denoise is a single model at one quality.** A larger variant exists in the conversion script but is not offered in the app, and the Models list in Settings has no row for the denoiser.
- **The colour calibration additions are simpler than darktable's.** Adaptation has no automatic illuminant detection (from the image, a grey patch or a colour checker) and adapts only to D65; the Channel Mixer has no Gray tab or per-row colourfulness and brightness controls; Gamut Compression has one Amount for all three channels. The Exposure eyedropper draws no rectangle while you drag and reads the picture before your local adjustments; Match Exposures by Histogram measures luminance only, ignoring masks, and doesn't account for Black Offset. Colour Grading's ⌥-drag preview reads the picture as finally shown, so later controls (presence, sharpening, the grading itself) shift it slightly from what the grading stage sees.
- **`latent-cli` renders and thumbnails leave out what needs a model:** model masks, Touch-up's smoothing, whitening and eyes, and Lens Blur. Dust spots and blemishes are heal patches and render everywhere. Exports, prints, contact sheets and slides carry everything.
- **Sensor dust has been tested on synthetic dust** and checked on only a few real photos. It looks for round dark shadows: foliage, birds and stars can be taken for dust, and faint spots at wide apertures can be missed; the rings are there to correct it. A dust map is keyed by the camera's make and model, so two bodies of the same model share one.
- **Touch-up works on faces at least 64 pixels wide** on a render about 4000 pixels across, seen more or less straight on; a turned head, glasses or hair across the face can defeat the landmarks. Smoothing acts on brightness only, so colour blotches stay; the teeth gate can catch a tongue or a lip highlight. Survey panes rebuild the regions when they open, which costs a moment per pane.
- **Subject masks with BiRefNet take about half a second each** on an M4 and longer on an M1, so a Survey of four such photos takes a few seconds to settle. Added models run on the GPU only.
- **Upright needs straight lines.** It reads edges on a render about a thousand pixels across, so a photo of nature, a soft or very dark one, or a building seen mostly as curves may give nothing to correct, and busy patterns (tiles, bars, text) can mislead it; Guided Upright is the fallback. Constrain Crop refits the crop when the transform or straighten changes, but a crop dragged by hand afterwards can still reach an empty edge. Flips are part of the edit, not of the photo's orientation like ⌘[ and ⌘], so they travel with Copy Settings' Transform Adjustments (or, in presets and checklists saved before those groups existed, with the crop), and Reset to Camera Orientation leaves them alone. A pasted Upright Mode is analysed on each photo by a background job, which waits for the graphics processor; if an export holds it, the mode is pasted without a correction and the status bar says to press Update on each photo.
- **Flat-field correction takes one reference per photo,** with no averaging of several frames and no automatic matching of a reference to photos by lens, focal length or aperture: you pick which photos it goes on. The gain map is smooth (64 cells across), so it corrects vignetting and colour casts but not dust or fixed-pattern noise. Its reference must stay where it was, relative to the photo, or keep its bytes, for another Mac (or a cleared cache) to make the map again; while it can't, the photo shows without the correction. Copy and paste don't carry it. Thumbnails show it only once the map has been made on this Mac.
- **Inpaint Opposed is for raws, and estimates one colour per photo.** The colour it rebuilds clipped areas with is the average of the unclipped pixels around all of them, so a photo with both a blown sky and blown skin gets one colour between them; a large area clipped in every channel gets that colour at about the clip level rather than real detail. Merged DNGs, JPEGs and other linear sources keep the blend. Scene-linear and DNG exports carry it.
- **Embedded DNG corrections are fitted to Latent's lens models** and are off until switched on. Distortion is fitted with a two-term radial polynomial (the DNG's sixth-order term and its tangential terms are dropped), vignetting with a three-term one, chromatic aberration comes only from a warp with separate colour planes, and an optical centre away from the frame's centre is treated as centred. Of the opcodes, only GainMap, FixVignetteRadial and WarpRectilinear are applied: WarpFisheye, bad-pixel lists, per-row and per-column offsets and the others are ignored. The shading map is laid over the demosaiced picture at 64 cells across, not over the raw mosaic at its own resolution. Maker-note corrections in Sony, Fujifilm, Olympus or Panasonic raws are not read. Lossy-JPEG and JPEG XL DNGs still can't be opened at all.
- **LMMSE has no refinement passes.** It is the plain estimate with a 9-pixel window, without RawTherapee's median refinement steps or darktable's colour smoothing, and like every demosaic choice it shows only at 100% and in exports. AMaZE, VNG4 and PPG are not offered.
- **Camera white balance presets come from LibRaw.** They appear only for cameras whose maker notes LibRaw reads presets from (none for the Nikon D750 the tests use), with no fine-tune steps, and the camera's custom and measured slots are left out.
- **Reflections needs a model you supply,** and none is offered: no reflection removal network has been vetted for its licence yet, so `convert_reflection_removal.py` has its pins left as `<pin me>`. The correction is a gain map at the model's output size (a few hundred pixels across), so it removes broad reflections well but can't take out a reflection's fine detail without softening the photo's own detail there; it is never sharper than the model's output. It is applied after the colour stage, so it is left out of scene-linear and DNG exports along with Lens Blur, and the model saw the photo's default rendering, not the edit.
- **Pet Eye** replaces everything inside its circle, so a circle larger than the pupil darkens iris too; its catchlight is a plain round highlight and its direction is fixed when the spot is made (or the switch is turned on), so a later quarter turn moves it.
- **Lens Blur has no refine brushes,** so a depth map that is wrong in one place (a gap between the arms, a glass of water) can only be fixed by moving the focus range or choosing another depth source. Without a depth model the depth is two planes, subject and background, so a scene that recedes blurs evenly rather than more with distance; no depth model is bundled, since the one that may be shipped has to be converted from its weights. The blur is worked out at most 16 pixels across on a shrunk copy, so a very large blur of fine bright detail can look slightly smoother than a real lens's.
- **Before/After and Reference view are Develop's alone,** with one reference at a time. A Before set with Copy After → Before lasts for the session and is not saved with the photo, and the Before always takes the After's crop and transform, so it never shows the uncropped original side by side (`\` still does). The reference pane cannot be edited in place: Swap makes it the Active photo. Loupe overlay guides are one across and one down, moved by dragging (no ⌘-drag to add more), and the layout image is placed with sliders rather than by dragging it.
- **The tone equalizer is one instance with one mask,** made from the whole photo's brightness; darktable's several instances, its choice of luminance estimators (it uses the working space's luminance, before any profile look) and its mask blending are not there, and it can't be limited to a local adjustment's mask. Its mask comes from a copy about 512 pixels across, refined at full resolution by each pixel's own brightness, so very fine bright detail can sit in a slightly different band at 100 % than in the preview. The scroll and drag tool works only while **On Image** is armed, not whenever the panel is open as in darktable. `latent-cli` and thumbnails render it, like the other sliders.
- **Hot pixel removal tests single photosites** against their same-colour neighbours on a Bayer mosaic. It does nothing for X-Trans sensors (which Latent does not render), for linear sources, or for defects two or more photosites wide; there is no overlay marking the pixels it fixed, only their count.
- **Fill's colour is approximate.** It is painted in camera space before the tone and colour edits, so the picked colour comes out as the photo's rendering makes it; there is no eyedropper to take a colour from the photo.
- **Lens Blur's Motion is a straight streak** in one direction for the whole frame, with no path to follow and no spin or zoom blur. Gaussian and Motion without a depth map blur everything, which is what a thumbnail or a `latent-cli` render shows of them.
- **No Depth Range mask.** Local adjustments can't yet be limited to a range of distances.
- **HDR editing is the HDR display switch and HDR Limit, not a separate mode.** There are no SDR Rendition settings (the SDR file is the tone curve without headroom), the histogram stays SDR (with a share of pixels above white), and single-channel grayscale files are converted to RGB when they open. Visualise HDR Ranges only shows something with HDR display on an HDR screen. Match Total Exposures works in the Library grid only, and Shift-double-click Auto only on Highlights, Shadows, Whites and Blacks.
- **Thumbnails ignore AI denoise**, since running the network in the background for every thumbnail would be too slow.
- **Limits:** 8 local adjustments of up to 7 parts each (a shape and 6 components), 5 colours per colour range, 32 spot patches (a brush stroke counts as one), 32 red-eye spots, 200 dust spots, 16 faces and 64 blemishes per image, 30 history steps.
- **One process version so far.** Edits record a [process version](Develop#process-versions), and the Update Process Version dialog is ready for the next rendering change, but changes made before process versions existed were not versioned: spot healing changed in September 2026, and Vibrance began sparing skin as intended on 2 November 2026, so heal patches and vibrance from before those dates render with the newer method. Both are Version 1.
- **Viewing aids are on screen only, and partly approximate.** Raw clipping at fit is judged on the binned preview, which averages pixels, so isolated clipped photosites show only when zoomed in, and the magnifier's own tile doesn't show it. Focus peaking reads the picture as shown, so sharpening, noise and fine texture add marks, and it has one threshold rather than darktable's several strengths. Colour assessment's border is a share of the view, not darktable's separate border and frame widths. The high-quality preview renders on the main thread after edits settle, so the window pauses briefly (longer for large sensors) each time it is made, and Survey's panes and a Develop second pane keep the binned preview.
- **Scope and picker limits.** The colour picker's area is a square of 5, 15 or 31 pixels, not a rectangle you draw; pinned samples are points or squares (eight at most), last only for the session and are not saved with the edit or compared against a reference. Area minima and maxima of hue are taken without wrapping round 0°. The vectorscope's guides have a fixed wedge width, and there is no false-colour or luma-only waveform.
- **Previous skips one frame's retouching.** Spot removal, red-eye, sensor dust and touch-up are not carried by Previous; copy and paste them with their groups ticked if you want them. Preset Amount blends slider values only (see [Presets and clipboard](Develop#presets-and-clipboard)).

## Accessibility

- Grid cells have no VoiceOver action to open an image; they do have one per rating.
- The waveform and vectorscope are named for VoiceOver but not described.
- Compare has no focus highlight.
- Placing patches, brush strokes, red-eye spots, dust spots, blemishes and masks by hand, moving the crop, drawing a straighten line or Upright guides, arranging a Custom sort and the magnifier need a pointer; Find Spots, Find Faces and Find Blemishes place theirs without one. See [Accessibility](Accessibility).

## Development

- **Golden-image tests don't cover AI noise reduction** or gain maps. Every other stage of the render is pinned against reference images, the touch-up stage with a fixed set of face regions; Core ML and Vision output varies between compute units and macOS versions, so neural denoise, the model masks and the face landmarks have only unit tests, and the dust detector is tested on synthetic scenes. What the magnifier and the slideshow draw on screen, and printed pages, aren't pictured by any test either.
- **Golden-image tests don't yet pin the new detail and effects stages** (grain, post-crop vignetting, sharpening Detail and Masking, noise reduction Detail, Contrast and Smoothness); they have render tests that check each control's effect and that off renders exactly as before.
- **Red-eye's thresholds are written twice,** in `RedEye.metal` and in `RedEyeTuning` (`RedEye.swift`), which Auto uses to decide an eye is red. They are kept in step by hand; no test ties them.
- Tests that render a raw need a sample that is not in the repository. `scripts/fetch_test_assets.sh` downloads the public-domain one the golden-image tests use, and with `--merge` the freely licensed brackets the Photo Merge tests use (about 390 MB), which skip without them; a few tests use the author's own samples and skip everywhere else, CI included.
- **`LATENT_RAW_INPROCESS=1` only works in debug builds.** `scripts/make_app.sh` always builds release, so no bundle it makes can decode in process.
