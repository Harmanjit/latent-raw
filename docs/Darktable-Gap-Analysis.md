# Latent versus darktable: gap analysis and integration scores

This document compares Latent at commit 038b5f4 (14 November 2026) with darktable 5.6.2, the latest stable release (4 October 2026). Eight researchers listed every darktable feature from the official user manual (docs.darktable.org) and the release notes. For each of the eight areas, a second agent checked every feature against Latent's code, and a third agent audited the result, correcting 14 rows and noting features the lists missed. Web pages were read; no darktable files were downloaded. No build or test was run for this document.

**Status.** Present: Latent has it and a user can reach it. Partial: part of it; the notes say what is missing. Missing: nothing comparable. Not applicable: it conflicts with a Latent design decision (macOS-only native app, no network or downloads, no import step, per-folder catalogs, no tethering, no Lua plug-in ecosystem, models imported by the user).

**Ease of integration** (Missing and Partial rows only): **5** trivial, reuses existing code, under a day; **4** easy, a small stage or panel following an existing pattern, 1–3 days; **3** moderate, a new pipeline stage or catalog feature with UI and tests, about a week; **2** hard, a new subsystem or deep pipeline/catalog change, 2–4 weeks; **1** very hard, research-grade or against Latent's architecture, months. Scores reflect Latent's real structure: a fixed-order Metal pipeline whose main colour kernel has no free argument slots, the EditStack format, the mask system and per-folder GRDB catalogs.

**Value**: how much a photographer would miss it, **5** an everyday tool, **1** niche. **Priority** in the tables below is ease × value (25 at best).

## Summary

darktable and Latent come at raw editing from opposite directions. darktable is a modular, scene-referred toolkit: its pixelpipe is a long chain of separate modules. You can reorder them, run several instances of any one, and mask each with drawn, parametric or raster masks combined through dozens of blend modes. That freedom is why it has tools like the tone equalizer, colour calibration (with chromatic adaptation and a colour-checker solver), diffuse or sharpen, and a choice of filmic, sigmoid or AgX tone mappers. The cost is a steep learning curve and many ways to reach the same result. Latent takes the Lightroom route instead. It has one fixed-order Metal pipeline: linear Rec.2020 through exposure, the tone ranges and the local adjustments, then a single Naka-Rushton sigmoid, then display-referred grading. The panels are familiar, every slider sits in one predictable place, and up to eight local-adjustment masks do the job that darktable's multiple instances do. Under the hood both apps are scene-referred. The difference is how much of the plumbing you are shown.

Latent is clearly ahead on the things that make a Mac photographer's day quicker. It has the Lightroom-style library: Survey and Compare culling, stacks, auto-stacking, an undoable custom sort, Find Duplicates and Verify Files. Per-folder catalogs travel with the folder, and sidecars are the source of truth, so a damaged catalog simply rebuilds itself. Photo Merge (HDR, panorama, HDR panorama) goes well beyond darktable's basic Create HDR. Latent also has content-aware Remove with object detection, depth-driven Lens Blur, reflection removal, Upright with guides, sensor dust and flat-field correction, and true EDR/HDR editing with gain-map and PQ export. Its output is polished too: export size limits, quality comparison, multi-preset export, a contact sheet PDF, and a slideshow with music.

darktable is ahead in three places. The first is depth of image science: the tone equalizer, highlight reconstruction that rebuilds colour from neighbouring pixels, capture sharpening, profiled denoise, 3D LUTs and a choice of tone mapper. The second is mask and blend flexibility: path masks, parametric channels and blend modes. The third is breadth of support: X-Trans, embedded lens corrections, a full-strength headless `darktable-cli`, styles management and GPX geotagging. X-Trans is the single biggest gap, because Fujifilm shooters currently get no usable raw path in Latent.

## Recommendations

### Add now (quick wins)

- **Compare a snapshot as Before** (ease 5, value 4). Today snapshots can only be restored or deleted. darktable's most-loved comparison is a saved snapshot shown beside the live edit. Add a "Compare as Before" item to each snapshot row in `HistoryPanel.swift`. It sets `beforeOverride = BeforeOverride(url:, stack: snapshot.stack)` (`EditorModel+BeforeAfter.swift`) and switches to a split layout. While you are there, make the split line draggable in `DevelopViews.swift`.

- **Wider text search in the filter bar** (ease 5, value 4). The search box only looks at file name, title and caption. Teach `LibraryFilter.matches` to check keywords (already passed in), camera, lens, creator, copyright and the relative path, with quoted phrases for exact matches. It is one function, and searching is something people do every day.

- **Read ratings and keywords from darktable and Lightroom sidecars** (ease 4, value 4). This is the most important feature for someone moving from darktable. Their `photo.NEF.xmp` files already hold years of ratings, colour labels and keywords, and Latent ignores them. In `Reconcile.swift`, when Latent first sees a file with no sidecar of its own, look for `basename.xmp` or `file.ext.xmp` beside it. Parse rating, label, `dc:subject` and `lr:hierarchicalSubject` with the existing XMP reader in `Catalog/Metadata.swift`, behind a one-time option in Settings › Folders.

- **Exposure eyedropper** (ease 4, value 3). Click an area and Latent sets Exposure so that area reaches a target lightness, as darktable's exposure picker does. Reuse the White Balance Selector's patch sampling (`WhiteBalanceTools.swift`, `PixelReadout.swift`), solve `EV = log2(target / patch)`, and put an eyedropper beside the Exposure slider in `ToneToolViews.swift`. No schema change is needed.

- **Proper preset (style) management** (ease 4–5, value 3). Add Import…/Export…, Rename, "Folder|Name" submenus, a Favourites section, and presets as bindable commands so they can have keyboard shortcuts. darktable users live in their styles. All of this sits in `PresetsPanel.swift`, `PresetStore`, `AppMenus.swift` and `ShortcutKeymap.swift`, and can follow the keymap import/export pattern in `KeyboardSettingsTab.swift`.

- **Apply a develop preset at export** (ease 4, value 3). This is darktable's "style on export": a watermark-free web look, or a B&W version, without touching the master edit. Add an optional preset (with Append/Replace) to the export preset. Merge it into the EditStack in `ExportWorker+Image.swift` before rendering, using the existing preset-amount blend, and add a picker in `ExportSheet+Extras.swift`.

- **Batch edit actions in the Library** (ease 4–5, value 3). Three small items: "Reset Edits" for a selection, "Sync last change to selection" (darktable's Ctrl+X), and a Paste Overwrite mode. The changed groups between the last two history steps come from extending `EditHistory.describeChange`. The rest goes through the existing undoable batch path (`Library+Undo`, `applyToSelectionOrEditor` in `ContentView.swift`).

- **Focus peaking and a raw clipping indicator** (ease 4, value 3). These are two view-only overlays, toggled like the existing clipping overlay (J). Focus peaking is a gradient-magnitude kernel on the displayed texture, which helps a lot when culling. The raw overexposure indicator compares the sensor values with `ImageSession`'s clip levels, so you can tell sensor clipping from tone-map clipping. Both live in `Present.metal`, `MetalImageView` and `EditorModel+Display.swift`, with no EditStack change.

- **Hot pixel removal** (ease 4, value 3). Long exposures and astro frames need it, and Sensor Dust doesn't cover single stuck photosites. Add a same-colour-neighbour median test on the sensor plane next to `WhiteBalance.metal`, a small EditStack module (absent means off), and a section in `DetailPanels.swift` with a "N pixels fixed" readout like the dust status.

- **Colour calibration extras: CAT illuminants, channel mixer, gamut compression** (ease 4, value 3). The CAT method and illuminant library, and a free 3×3 channel mixer with normalise, can both be folded into `cameraToWorking` on the CPU in `RenderPipeline+ColourControls.swift`, so the full colorAndTone kernel needs no new buffer. Gamut compression is a per-pixel soft chroma squeeze after stage 9, with its amount packed into a spare `ColourExtras` lane. The UI goes in the Calibration panel (`ColourPanels.swift`).

- **"Colour only / Lightness only" blending for masks** (ease 4, value 3). This is the most useful slice of darktable's blend modes. It lets you, for example, brighten a face without shifting its colour. Pack the mode into the unused `LocalAdjustGPU.info.w` (`LocalAdjustments.swift`), recombine input chroma with output luminance (or the reverse) in `ColorPipeline.metal`, and mirror the change in `LocalDetailStage.swift`.

- **Borders and enlarge canvas at export** (ease 4, value 3). darktable's framing and enlarge canvas modules, done Latent's way, as an export step next to the watermark. Pad the resized image in `Exporter+Output.swift` with a size, aspect ratio, colour and optional inner line, and add a section in `ExportSheet+Extras.swift`. Prints can reuse it in `PageRenderer.swift`.

- **`latent-cli export` that uses the saved edit** (ease 4, value 3). `latent-cli render` ignores the photo's sidecar today, so headless batch export like `darktable-cli` isn't possible. Open the folder's Catalog (as the merge commands already do), load each EditStack, and drive the existing `Exporter`/`ExportWorker`. Accept `--preset`, `--format`, `--long-edge` and `--out-dir` flags.

### Plan next (worth the effort)

- **X-Trans rendering** (ease 3, value 5). Fujifilm users currently can't use Latent at all. The quick route is to have the XPC decoder run LibRaw's own X-Trans demosaic (`clibraw_shim.cpp`) and return a `LinearPlane` through the existing linear-source path (`RawFile+LinearSource.swift`). A native Markesteijn Metal kernel can come later. Audit the Bayer-only code too: dust, flat-field, HDR merge, DNG export and CA estimation.

- **Tone equalizer** (ease 3, value 4). This is darktable's signature tool: nine EV bands driven by an edge-aware (guided-filter) luminance mask, so you can compress a scene's range without the flat look a global curve gives. Run it as its own scene-linear pass before colorAndTone, because that kernel has no free slots. Reuse the blur passes from `LocalContrast.metal`, draw the curve over the mask histogram (`HistogramCalculator.swift`), and show the mask through the `MaskOverlay` path. The guided-filter kernel could later give the Luminance Range mask a "smooth" option.

- **Highlight reconstruction: inpaint opposed** (ease 3, value 4). Blown skies and skin currently turn white. Estimating colour from the surrounding unclipped pixels keeps them blue or warm. This is a new camera-space stage before the colour matrix, with tile-border overlap, and a mode picker in the Highlight Reconstruction panel. Keep the current method for old edits via an optional field or `ProcessVersion`.

- **Capture sharpening** (ease 3, value 4). A few Richardson–Lucy iterations with a Gaussian PSF straight after demosaic, plus an automatic radius estimate (in the style of `ChromaticAberrationEstimator`). It recovers real detail rather than adding unsharp-mask edges. It needs a new EditStack module, a "Capture" section in `DetailPanels.swift` and export tile margins.

- **3D LUT support (.cube / .3dl / HaldCLUT)** (ease 3, value 4). This is a big draw for people with film-emulation packs. The kernel already samples 3D textures for soft proof and profiles. Parse the files with the `SoftProofLUT.makeTexture` pattern, copy imported LUTs into a folder as DCP import does, and list them as Creative profiles with Amount in `ProfilePicker.swift`.

- **Default settings and auto-apply preset rules** (ease 3, value 4). A rule table (camera, lens, ISO range, raw or not → preset) applied to photos that have never been edited. The care is in applying it everywhere an absent edit is read: `EditorModel+Opening.swift`, `EditedThumbnailRenderer.swift`, export and latent-cli. Thumbnails also need invalidating when a rule changes.

- **Path (polygon/Bézier) mask** (ease 3, value 4). This is the mask most often asked for after the brush. Rasterise it on the CPU into a brush slice with `CGPath` plus a feather blur, exactly as brush strokes are (`LocalsGPUPlan` in `LocalAdjustments.swift`), so the kernel doesn't change. Most of the work is node editing in a new overlay next to `MaskShapeOverlay.swift`. Rotatable ellipses are a small add-on in the same area.

- **Embedded lens corrections (DNG opcodes, then Sony)** (ease 3, value 4). Mirrorless lenses that rely on software correction get no profile when Lensfun lacks them. Parse WarpRectilinear and FixVignetteRadial (and GainMap, for flat-field) in `clibraw_shim.cpp`, carry them in `SourceMetadata`, and turn them into a "Built-in (camera)" profile in LensKit.

- **A second tone mapper (AgX), plus sigmoid skew and preserve hue** (ease 3, value 3). The EditStack Tone module already has a `method` field set to "sigmoid", so the schema slot exists. The real cost is the CPU mirrors of the curve (`MaskComponents.swift`, `ToneRanges.swift`, the inverse in `RenderedDNGExport.swift`) and gain-map export. Parameters have to be packed into existing uniforms. AgX is more worthwhile than filmic, which darktable no longer uses by default.

- **Capture time shift and GPX geotagging** (ease 3, value 3). An "Edit Capture Time…" sheet that writes the new time to the sidecar and makes Reconcile prefer it. Then GPS columns (a `Schema.swift` migration), an offline GPX parser with time interpolation, and export writing the coordinates. It is fully offline, so it respects the no-network rule.

### Consider later or skip

- **Reorderable pipeline, module-order presets, and multiple instances of global modules** (ease 1). These fight the fused colorAndTone kernel and the single-valued EditStack. Keep growing the per-mask slider set instead, so a Whole Image mask acts as a second instance.
- **Diffuse or sharpen** (ease 2, value 3). It is impressive, but it is an iterative multi-scale subsystem with heavy GPU and tiling costs. Wait until capture sharpening and the tone equalizer have landed.
- **Colour-checker calibration** (ease 2, value 2). Valuable for reproduction work but niche. Do it after the CAT and channel mixer exist, so the solver has a place to store its result.
- **Profiled denoise and astrophoto NLM** (ease 2–3, value 2–3). NAFNet AI noise reduction already covers most of the need.
- **The full arithmetic and contrast blend-mode suite, blend colourspaces, fulcrum and reverse order** (ease 3–4, value 1). These have little point without darktable's module stack. The colour/lightness subset above gives most of the benefit.
- **Composite module** (ease 2, value 1). It needs a second full render with dependency tracking.
- **Liquify, frequency-separation retouch, MIDI and game controllers, translations, local copies, zoomable lighttable** (ease 1–2). Each is a whole subsystem for a small audience, and local copies also fight the in-folder catalog design.
- **Foveon and SuperCCD support, extra demosaic algorithms, XCF/PFM/PPM/LaTeX output** (low value). These are niche, and LibRaw has dropped Foveon.
- **Map view, online geocoding, Piwigo or email export, tethering, Lua scripting** (not applicable: no network, no tethering, no plugin ecosystem).

## What Latent has that darktable does not

- **Photo Merge:** HDR, panorama and HDR panorama with auto-align, adjustable deghosting with an overlay, a choice of reference frame and Auto Settings, all written as linear DNGs. darktable's Create HDR is a basic merge with no panorama.
- **A content-aware Remove tool:** PatchMatch fill with Detect Objects (SAM 2.1), alongside Heal and Clone.
- **AI and computational extras darktable lacks:** depth-driven Lens Blur, reflection removal, automatic red-eye and pet-eye fixes, blemish finding, Touch-up skin smoothing, Select by Class and sky masks, and Upright assistance.
- **Sensor dust detection, and flat-field correction from your own reference shot.**
- **Guided Upright:** you draw two to four guide lines on top of the Auto, Level, Vertical and Full modes.
- **True HDR editing on EDR displays:** HDR Limit headroom, Visualise HDR Ranges, and gain-map or 10-bit PQ export to HEIC and AVIF.
- **Lightroom-style culling and organising:** Survey and Compare with synced zoom, stacks with Auto-Stack by Capture Time, an undoable custom sort, Find Duplicates, Verify Files, and RAW+JPEG pairs that move and rename together.
- **Catalogs that live inside the folder:** moving a folder in Finder never breaks anything. Sidecars are the source of truth, and a damaged database rebuilds itself from them.
- **Wide Library undo:** it covers ratings, keywords, pasted edits, moves, copies, renames, stacks and custom sort.
- **Match Total Exposures** across a series, and **Auto Import** from a watched folder with an optional preset.
- **Export conveniences:** a file-size limit, a 2–4 quality comparison at 100%, multi-preset export in one run, Export with Previous, and adding results back to the catalog stacked with their originals.
- **Soft-proofing extras:** Simulate Paper & Ink, proof copies saved as snapshots, and separate destination and monitor gamut warnings.
- **Contact Sheet PDF, and a slideshow** with transitions, captions and music.
- **A live Loupe on a second display** at that screen's own resolution and headroom, plus Reference View and the Loupe overlay (grid, guides, layout image).
- **Lightroom-style targeted adjustment tools** for the Tone Curve and HSL, Point Colour, and dragging on the histogram to change tones.
- **A native macOS feel:** the system print panel, ColorSync display handling, VoiceOver labels, Reduce Motion and Increase Contrast support, and a keymap editor with conflict warnings and import/export.
